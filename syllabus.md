Course Description: Use Catalog Course Description

This seminar teaches students to use agentic AI — systems that plan, use tools, retrieve sources, and act over multiple steps — as instruments of theological and religious-studies research, while critically examining the theological and ethical questions autonomous AI raises. Students learn to design a research process: an agent that retrieves primary sources (Retrieval-Augmented Generation, RAG), cross-references them, drafts with citations, critiques its own output, and — crucially — is evaluated so its claims are checked rather than trusted. The course is organized around AI as both a scholarly instrument ("agentic AI for studying theology") and a theological subject ("what theology says about agentic AI"), built on three technical pillars: Agentic AI · RAG · Evaluation. Each student (or pair) develops, executes, evaluates, and defends an original agentic-AI research project.

Course Learning Outcomes: [If a Christ at the Core course, use the CATC learning outcomes from the advisory document, and additional learning goals for the course may be included as well but labeled as course goals.]

Students will be able to . . .

1. Explain what turns an LLM into an agent (tool use, memory, plan→act→observe, autonomy) and build a multi-step, self-critiquing research agent. [Agentic AI]

2. Build a retrieval-augmented (RAG) pipeline over a theological corpus so the agent cites real sources, and diagnose where grounding fails. [RAG]

3. Design and run an evaluation of a RAG/agent system — retrieval quality, groundedness/faithfulness, answer relevance, task success, and cost — using golden datasets, LLM-as-judge, and human review, while knowing each method's limits. [Evaluation]

4. Design a researchable theological question with a matching agentic method and evaluation plan.

5. Analyze the theological questions autonomy raises — personhood, imago Dei, agency, responsibility, spiritual authority — and report AI-assisted scholarship honestly.

Assignments: Describe course assignments and the assigned weight towards the final grade

- Interest statement (5%, Week 1): A one-paragraph statement of the theological research interest the student will pursue all term.
- Research proposal + pitch (20%, Week 7 midpoint gate): A 1–2 page proposal plus a 5-minute pitch converting curiosity into a researchable question, an agent design, and — required — an evaluation plan (metrics, golden set, human check).
- Preliminary results + evaluation report (15%, Week 12): An early-warning checkpoint in which students run a real evaluation of their own system (scorecard: groundedness, citation checks, task success, bias spot-checks) and defend it in peer review.
- Final paper (30%, Week 14): An honest scholarly argument — a real finding or a well-argued negative result — with a documented, reproducible RAG/agent pipeline, verified claims, and theological/ethical reflection.
- Final presentation (15%, Week 14): A symposium presentation to the cohort and invited faculty, with Q&A and a closing reflection.
- Participation & project touchpoints (15%, weekly): Engagement in discussion, labs, peer review, and mentor check-ins.

Every deliverable must include the required agentic-AI use disclosure appendix.

Class Schedule:

14 weeks · one 100-minute session/week (~40 min concept & discussion · ~40 min hands-on lab · ~20 min project touchpoint).

Phase 1 — Foundations
- Week 1 — Framing: "AI for Theology." Instrument vs. subject; survey of the AI/religion intersection; preview the three pillars. Deliverable: interest statement. Readings: a survey on AI & religion (e.g., Beth Singler); course intro notes.
- Week 2 — From LLMs to agents [Agentic AI]. Next-token prediction, embeddings, hallucination; tool use, memory, the plan→act→observe loop, autonomy. Readings: Wolfram (selections); Anthropic, "Building Effective Agents"; Weng, "LLM-Powered Autonomous Agents." Optional: Yao et al., "ReAct."
- Week 3 — Sources and the theological corpus. Digital primary sources; copyright, canon, translation politics; "the data is theologically loaded." Hands-on: load the term corpus. Readings: Claire Clivaz on digital humanities & biblical studies (selection).

Phase 2 — Technical Core: Agentic AI · RAG · Evaluation
- Week 4 — Retrieval-Augmented Generation [RAG]. Embeddings, semantic search, chunking, the retrieve→augment→generate loop; failure modes. Hands-on: build an "ask-your-corpus" RAG agent. Readings: Lewis et al. (2020).
- Week 5 — Building a research agent [Agentic AI]. Planning, reflection/self-critique; a two-agent "researcher + critic/fact-checker" setup. Hands-on: extend Week 4 into a planning + reflection agent with a fact-checker. Readings: Shinn et al., "Reflexion" (2023); Wang et al., "A Survey on LLM-based Autonomous Agents" (2023). Guest slot recommended.
- Week 6 — Evaluation: how do you know it works? [Evaluation]. RAG evaluation (retrieval precision/recall, groundedness/faithfulness, citation verification), agent evaluation (task success, trajectory correctness, cost/latency), methods (golden datasets, LLM-as-judge and its pitfalls, human review), and theology-specific angles (doctrinal fidelity, translation sensitivity, bias across traditions). Hands-on: build a golden Q&A set and report a scorecard. Readings: Es et al., "RAGAS"; Liu et al., "G-Eval"; Zheng et al., "MT-Bench" (selections).
- Week 7 — Research design & proposal (midpoint gate). Scope, method, evidence; the evaluation/verification plan; reproducibility and documenting agentic-AI use. Deliverable: 1–2 page proposal + 5-min pitch (must include an evaluation plan).

Phase 3 — Domain Deep-Dives + Project Launch
- Week 8 — AI and hermeneutics. Multi-step reasoning vs. genuine understanding; authorial intent, tradition, interpretive community. Hands-on: projects formally begin; first evaluated agent runs. Readings: Gadamer, Truth and Method (selections); a piece on machine "understanding."
- Week 9 — Theology of agentic AI. Imago Dei, personhood, consciousness; agency and autonomy, moral status, responsibility; AI and idolatry; eschatology. Hands-on: mentored project check-in. Readings: Herzfeld (2023); Dorobantu on AI & personhood; Vatican, Rome Call for AI Ethics (2020) / Antiqua et Nova (2025).
- Week 10 — Ethics, bias, and the limits of autonomy. Training-data bias; fabricated citations; agentic-specific risks; designing human oversight; bias-aware evaluation. Hands-on: add an explicit verification/oversight step to each eval plan. Readings: Bender et al., "Stochastic Parrots" (2021); Crawford, Atlas of AI (2021, selections).

Phase 4 — Mentored Research Execution
- Week 11 — Build sprint (studio). Execute the agentic pipeline; structured trace debugging with mentors circulating.
- Week 12 — Evaluate & critique [Evaluation, applied]. Run a real evaluation of your own project; peer-review swap + mentor stress-test. Deliverable: preliminary results + evaluation report.
- Week 13 — Write-up & communication. Presenting agentic work with integrity — goals, tools, traces, eval results, failures, human oversight. Draft final paper + slides.

Week 14 — Symposium. Final presentations (cohort + invited faculty), Q&A, and a closing reflection. Deliverable: final paper + presentation.
